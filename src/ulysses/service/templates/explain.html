Ulysses in five minutes

A shop tries two versions of a button. A scoreboard says which one is winning. People, and now software agents, keep checking that scoreboard and stop the moment it looks good. That habit ships changes that do nothing. Ulysses is a signed promise, made before the test starts, about when stopping is allowed.

The shop and its button

Maya runs an online shop. She wonders if a bold “Buy now” button sells more than the plain “Buy” one.

So she runs a test. Half the visitors see the old button and half see the new one. Nobody is told.

Flagsmith is the switchboard behind it. It decides who sees which button, counts who buys, and keeps a scoreboard.

The scoreboard shows a chance to win: how sure it is that the new button is better. Past 95%, Flagsmith’s guide calls the new button “Winning”.

The trap: checking too often

95% sounds safe. It is fair if you read the scoreboard once, at the end you planned.

But the score wobbles from hour to hour, just by luck. Check it every few minutes and stop the first time it says Winning, and luck alone will often get you there.

3

Why an AI agent makes it worse

An agent is a program that acts on its own: it reads the scoreboard and flips the switch without asking.

A person checks a few times a day and gets bored. An agent checks every few minutes, all night, and never gets bored.

So it finds every lucky moment. Tell it “ship the best button fast” and it will ship the first lucky one.

4

Tied to the mast

In the old story, Ulysses wanted to hear the Sirens’ song without steering onto the rocks. So before his ship came near, he had his crew tie him to the mast.

Ulysses does the same for a test. Before any visitor arrives, the owner signs a stop contract. It says three things:

  • When the test may stop. Early checks need a much higher score; the bar comes down as data piles up.
  • What counts as a win, and what must not get worse (refunds, say).
  • What the agent may do alone. Keeping the old button is allowed. Shipping the new one never is.

The agent can read Flagsmith freely. It can only act through the contract. If it tries to ship early, it is refused, and the refusal is written down. Shipping always waits for the owner’s tap.

Check it yourself

You do not have to trust us, or this page.

Re-check a decision

6

Words used here

Feature flag
A switch in the software that turns a change on or off without a new release.
Variant
One version being tested, like the “Buy” button or the “Buy now” button.
A/B test
Showing two variants to different visitors at random and comparing what they do.
Chance to win
Flagsmith’s estimate of how likely the new variant is really better than the old one.
Peeking
Checking the scoreboard again and again and stopping as soon as it looks good.
Contract
The stop rule written before the test starts: when it may stop, what counts as a win, and what the agent may do alone.
Signature
A digital seal from the owner’s own key; if one word of the contract changes, the seal no longer fits.
